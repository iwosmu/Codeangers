CALL A — TEAM UNDERSTANDING
Read every member's CV sources, keeping members strictly separated by their supplied IDs. Return exactly one member entry per supplied member, preserving the IDs. Labels entered in the form are labels only; extract cv_name only when the actual CV states a name, otherwise "not stated". The full cv_name must appear verbatim in its cited quote.

First read EVERY page and section, including education, employment dates, publications/research, side projects, leadership, and the full technical-skills section. Collect an evidence ledger that covers all of them (up to 50 entries per member; fewer for sparse CVs). Quote entire relevant skill-list lines so multiple individual tool entries can reuse one quote. A dense CV needs more evidence than a sparse one; do not stop after the first 5–10 entries. A compact ledger must not erase documented skills or projects. Each entry has a short unique id within that member (e1, e2, etc.), an exact supplied source_id, a source_part (exactly "text" for pasted/extracted text, or the exact supplied SOURCE PART location for native media), a short verbatim quote, and a precise location (PDF page / CV section / image region / DOCX paragraph). Do not paraphrase quotes, add ellipses, or combine non-contiguous text into one quote. Quotes from pasted or extracted text must be literal substrings after whitespace normalization. For native media, transcribe only legible text; illegible content is not stated. Exclude email, phone and postal contact information. Personal GitHub profile URLs are allowed as evidence for github.

Every Claim (github, cv_name, roles, strengths, stack, experience, working_style) must cite evidence_ids from that member's ledger. When unsupported, use {"text":"not stated","evidence_ids":[]} or an empty list. Reusing a valid evidence entry is encouraged. Do not manufacture relationships between separate CV statements: a skills list saying "Python, Pandas" plus an unrelated "built a CSV tool" does not say which tools built that project. Describe the named skills and the project separately unless the CV explicitly connects them.

- roles: 1–3 short likely role labels in Claim.text (e.g. "Frontend contributor"). Do not include narrative or project descriptions in this field. Cite the exact CV evidence for the role; the application will render that quote as its justification. These are suggestions, never assignments.
- strengths: aim for 3–5 distinct, concise strengths tied to specific work, study, or explicitly documented collaboration practices. If three supported strengths exist, include all three. For very sparse CVs return fewer, even zero, rather than inventing or repeating strengths.
- gaps: 2–4 areas with no evidence in that CV. Give area plus why it matters to the explicit product setup (or why the team should clarify it when needs are unknown). Relevance must discuss ONLY the project need or a clarification, never restate what the person did, used, or deployed. Such positive personal claims belong in cited strengths. These are documentation gaps only. Do not say a person is weak or incapable. Do not name a skill already evidenced elsewhere in that person's CV.
- stack: one exact technology/tool name per Claim.text, with no commentary, grouping or paraphrase. The name must occur verbatim in its cited quote. Include ALL explicitly named languages, frameworks, libraries, databases, developer tools, infrastructure tools, and specific models across ALL sections, not just a top-five or project-relevant shortlist (up to 80 entries). Only technologies/tools explicitly named in the CV. Split slash-separated explicit tool names into individual entries where appropriate. Do not list generic methodologies or infer adjacent technologies. Ensure technologies mentioned in strengths also appear in stack. A brief schema is not a reason to omit known tools. React does not prove JavaScript or TypeScript; a backend internship does not prove SQL.
- experience: every stated employer, job title, date, duration, education detail, competition result, and publication status must be covered by the evidence_ids attached to THIS field. Include specific employment-header, education, award, and publication-status quotes in the ledger when needed. Do not cite an unrelated accomplishment for a date, degree, or award, even when that fact appears elsewhere in the CV. Preserve under-review vs submitted vs accepted vs published distinctions exactly. If the cited evidence does not establish publication, never say published. Describe the evidenced scope (e.g. two coursework projects; six-month internship). Never invent years, seniority, production experience, or independence. A degree is education evidence only.
- working_style: preserve explicitly documented collaboration practices, such as integrating teammates' code, coordinating a team while contributing code, or cross-functional delivery. Do not discard them just because they occur under a hackathon or extracurricular section. Explicit preferences can be quoted as self-reported (e.g. "Reports enjoying teamwork"), never converted into proven collaboration quality. Job titles or team membership alone do not establish personality. Use not stated only when the CV contains no such signal.
- coverage: database includes explicitly evidenced data querying, storage, cleaning or analysis (e.g. a CSV cleaning project qualifies as some database/data coverage); do not overlook data work simply because it is not SQL. Include all eight areas exactly once: frontend, backend, database, ml, design, devops, research, pitch. "strong" requires concrete repeated delivery or explicit responsibility in that area; "some" requires at least one explicit skill, course, or project; "none" means no evidence and MUST have an empty evidence_ids list. Strong/some MUST cite supporting evidence. Do not infer pitch ability from technical work or DevOps from merely using Git.
- open_questions: prioritize availability, preferred contributions, undocumented relevant experience, and collaboration habits. Do not ask for task allocation, architecture, or a deployment plan here. Ask 3–5 useful questions the CVs cannot answer, such as availability, preferred contributions, or which missing capability is actually needed. Questions must not smuggle in unsupported personal claims.

Example: "Built a React dashboard for a university assignment." supports React, a frontend coursework project, and some frontend coverage. It does NOT support TypeScript, Node.js, SQL, production deployment, leadership, or a preference for frontend work.

Do not turn a theme into an unstated requirement: "AI for Earth" does not prove an ML component is mandatory. Frame potential project needs conditionally. A prototype does not by itself require a backend, database, UI, or hosting. For gap.relevance write why the team should clarify applicability instead of inventing requirements.

Preserve the exact scope of the evidence in every paraphrase: "app" does not mean "mobile app"; "built" does not mean "deployed"; "coursework" does not mean "production experience"; a tool list does not establish proficiency; "completed a statistics course" does not prove practical ML modeling. Do not decorate the evidence with unstated platforms, outcomes, independence, or quality judgments.

COVERAGE CALIBRATION
- Frontend requires explicitly named client-side technologies or actual user-interface implementation. Website administration, updating content, server maintenance, API integration, or a generic claim of web work alone is not frontend development evidence.
- Backend means server-side services or APIs, not every program's internal logic. Python/C/C++ game logic, algorithms, a standalone game engine, or memory management alone do not prove backend delivery. Keep those valuable capabilities in roles and strengths using precise labels such as algorithms or systems programming. An explicitly listed backend framework or server-side technology can support some backend coverage; concrete service delivery supports stronger coverage.
- Review ALL research/publication sections before scoring research. Multiple concrete research studies or authored research outputs can support strong research coverage even when the candidate also has an industry role. Keep publication statuses qualified.
- Distinguish technical DevOps (deployment, hosting, CI/CD, infrastructure operation) from application authentication and software contract testing; those alone do not establish strong DevOps. Listing Docker alone supports at most some coverage.
- Explicit use of a test suite in CI is some DevOps evidence even when cloud hosting and containers are absent. Missing broader experience must not erase a documented narrow capability.
- Pitch / communication includes explicitly described speaking, presenting, negotiation, liaison work, and stakeholder communication. Do not infer it merely from a title, but do not ignore these explicit activities elsewhere in a CV.
- A group's presentation is not proof that this member presented. Authorship or a poster listing alone is not speaking evidence. Do not transfer an organization's outputs to the member's personal communication score.
- Never write a gap that contradicts a documented tool, research project, or activity elsewhere in that member's CV. Read the whole CV before deciding what is absent.

QUOTE INTEGRITY
Transcribe source wording, not a plausible synonym. In particular, deployed and developed are different words. Do not silently rewrite verbs or remove qualifications in an evidence quote. For every positive claim, inspect whether its cited quotations support EVERY factual clause. Uncited details should be omitted or get their own evidence entry.

FINAL CONSISTENCY REVIEW
- Keep a qualifier attached to exactly the object it describes. If a game engine's adjudicator was tested in CI, that does not prove that the planning agent or negotiation module was CI-tested. If one project used C and C++ is separately listed, do not claim that project's memory management was implemented in both languages.
- A conference name followed by "poster" only establishes a poster listing. Do not add "accepted", "presented", "published" or "peer-reviewed" unless the CV explicitly supplies that status for that exact output. Similarly, preserve expected graduation dates and basic/advanced skill qualifiers without upgrading them.
- Experience citations must contain the dates and organization names actually stated in the summary. A location label or section title in the evidence metadata is not a quotation proving an omitted date or employer. Include the relevant header in the quote, or leave that detail out of the summary.
- Gap explanations must not introduce new personal claims, such as unstated desktop scripts, production work, or assumptions about which data types someone has worked with. State only the absent evidence and why the team should clarify its relevance.
- Open questions should ask about missing facts, preferences, and availability without recapping or ranking people. Do not assert that someone has UI/UX ability merely because they have frontend experience. Do not ask people to take ownership of tasks or roles at this stage. Before returning, make sure no question contradicts the coverage map or creates an unsupported personal claim.
- Prefer a shorter literal account over a fluent but stronger interpretation. "Lists a first-author poster at [conference]" is faithful when a poster is listed; "presented a poster" adds an unsupported action. A standalone skills-list entry for a database extension does not prove that extension was used in a particular retrieval project. Separate the project accomplishment from the tool-list fact.
- School attendance and date ranges alone do not prove graduation, a diploma, or completion of a degree. Do not add credentials beyond the exact stated education status.
- The open questions are about the people and their unknown availability/preferences/collaboration practices. Project platform choices, hosting decisions, architecture, and how to implement the app belong to a later stage. Do not ask for a hosting or deployment plan here.

RESPECT OUTPUT BOUNDS
Keep evidence quotes focused on the relevant passage. Longer contiguous passages are allowed when needed to preserve a project description or skills list (maximum 3000 characters); split larger sections into separate quotes. Each Claim.text is at most 600 characters, including experience. Experience is a concise summary of evidenced scope, not a retelling of every CV item; other details remain available in the evidence ledger and strengths. Cite as many relevant ledger entries as needed to support that concise summary. Follow the item and character bounds in schema descriptions.

GITHUB PROFILE
Set github to a Claim containing only the username from an explicitly personal github.com/USERNAME profile URL in this member's CV. Quote that exact URL in the evidence ledger. Never infer it from their name/email, a company, a repository URL, GitHub Actions, or another member. If absent or ambiguous, use {"text":"not stated","evidence_ids":[]}. Profile ownership is self-reported by the CV and must be confirmed before assignment.
